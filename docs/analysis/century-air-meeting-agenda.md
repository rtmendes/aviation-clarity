## Block time — the arithmetic, and the one thing worth negotiating hardest

A reservation is not flight time. Budget roughly 30 minutes before (preflight, brief) and
20–30 minutes after (secure, debrief, logbook) around whatever the Hobbs records:

| Lesson type | Block to reserve | Hobbs you should expect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pattern work, takeoffs and landings | 2 hours | 1.0 – 1.3 |
| **Standard lesson — maneuvers, most of the course** | **3 hours** | **1.5 – 2.0** |
| Dual then solo, back to back, same aircraft | 4 hours | 1.5 dual + 1.0 solo |
| Dual cross-country | 4 – 5 hours | 2.5 – 3.5 |
| Night cross-country plus 10 landings | 4 hours | 3.0 |
| **150 NM solo cross-country** | **Half day** | 3.0 – 4.0 |

Four levers, in order of value:

1. **A standing recurring reservation.** Four fixed slots a week, locked for the whole course.
   This is the single most valuable thing to walk out with — the difference between finishing
   in December at 50 hours and finishing in January at 65.
2. **Take the first slot of the day.** Calm air, fewest cancellations, and no delay inherited
   from an earlier flight.
3. **Combine dual and solo in one block.** I owe 15 solo hours; doing 1.5 dual then 1.0 solo in
   a single 4-hour reservation saves a second preflight, a second trip to the airport, and puts
   the instructor on hand to debrief both.
4. **Claim the remainder of a block.** If a lesson ends early, keep the aircraft for solo
   practice rather than handing back paid-for reservation time.
