# Air Fleet Training Systems — Part 141 private pilot budget, Pipistrel Alpha Trainer

**Source documents:** Air Fleet Training Systems Course Estimates, last updated 20 October 2025,
pages 1 and 9 (photographed 10 September 2026). **School:** Air Fleet Training Systems, Essex
County Airport (KCDW), Fairfield NJ — [airfleettraining.com](https://www.airfleettraining.com/).

**Status of this document:** the rate card below is decoded and arithmetically verified against
the school's own printed course totals. The regulatory minimums are cited to primary sources.
**Three load-bearing assumptions are NOT verified and are flagged in §6** — the largest is the
examining-authority claim, which Air Fleet's own price sheet appears to contradict.

## 2. The template is an estimate, not a floor

Air Fleet's printed PIAT course assumes **60 hours dual + 5 hours solo = 65 hours**. Their own
disclaimer, printed on both pages, says what that figure is:

> "The course costs listed above are an estimate of what the average student will pay. Students
> are not guaranteed to finish training in the hour quantities listed. The actual cost of
> training is based on the number of hours flown, and the type of aircraft flown."

So the 60 hours is an average, not a syllabus minimum. The regulatory floor is set by
[14 CFR Part 141 Appendix B](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/appendix-B_to_part_141):

| Part 141 Appendix B, private pilot airplane single-engine | Minimum |
| --- | --- |
| Ground training (§3) | **35 hours** |
| Flight training total (§4) | **35 hours** |
| — of which, from a certificated flight instructor (§4(b)(1)) | **20 hours** |
| — of which, solo flight training (§5(a)) | **5 hours** |
| Solo cross-country | one 100 NM flight, 3 landing points, 3 takeoffs/landings at a controlled airport |
| Night | 3 hr, incl. one >100 NM cross-country and 10 takeoffs/landings |
| Instrument | 3 hr by reference to instruments |
| Practical test preparation | 3 hr |

Two things follow. **Air Fleet's template carries 30 hours of padding above the Part 141 floor** —
that is the money you are asking about. And the Part 141 floor is genuinely lower than Part 61's
40 hours under [61.109](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/61.109), with a 100 NM solo
cross-country instead of Part 61's 150 NM.

**The ground school reading is correct.** Appendix B requires 35 hours of ground training. Air
Fleet's course meets it with the ~30-hour self-paced course ($427.57) plus 10 hours of live
classroom instruction ($750). Only the 10 hours is live instructor time. That is why the ground
line is $1,177.57 and not 35 × $75 = $2,625.

## 4. The comparison that matters

Held to identical hours and an identical 35 dual / 15 solo split, school invoice only:

| Option | 50 hr school invoice | vs Century Air |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| **Century Air, Cessna 172P, Part 61** | **$13,498** | — |
| Air Fleet, Pipistrel Alpha Trainer, Part 141 | $14,655.50 | **+$1,157.50** |
| Air Fleet, Cessna 172S steam gauge, Part 141 | $18,035.50 | +$4,537.50 |

**At equal hours the Pipistrel is not cheaper than Century Air's 172P — it is about $1,160 more.**
The Pipistrel's $195 wet rate is essentially identical to the 172P's $196 solo rate, and the
$280 dual rate is $2 below the 172P's $282. The difference is that Air Fleet adds two charges
Century Air does not: the per-lesson briefing charge and 10 hours of mandatory classroom
instruction.

**The saving at Air Fleet comes from Part 141's lower hour floor, not from the aircraft — and
since Century Air also offers Part 141, that floor is available at either school.** So the
comparison that actually decides this is not aircraft or regulation. It is price against cadence.

### The trade that decides it

| Hours (dual/solo) | Century 172P | Air Fleet Pipistrel | Gap | Gap in dual hours |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 50 hr (35/15) | $13,498 | $14,656 | +$1,158 | 4.1 hr |
| 45 hr (30/15) | $12,088 | $13,179 | +$1,091 | 3.9 hr |
| 40 hr (30/10) | $11,108 | $12,128 | +$1,020 | 3.6 hr |
| 35 hr (25/10) | $9,698 | $10,651 | +$953 | 3.4 hr |

Air Fleet is roughly **$1,000–$1,160 more expensive at any matched hour count**. At their $280/hr
dual rate, that entire gap is **about four hours of dual instruction**.

Cadence is the largest single driver of hour overrun — skill decay between lessons is what turns
a 50-hour course into a 65-hour one, and re-flying last week's lesson costs $280 an hour. So:

> **If Century Air's scheduling costs you more than about 4 extra dual hours across the whole
> course, Air Fleet is the cheaper school despite the higher rates.** Century Air at 54 hours
> costs the same as Air Fleet at 50.

Four hours is a very low bar. It is one cancelled week, or two lessons repeated because the gap
between them was too long.

| Comparison | Cost | Delta |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Century Air 172P, Part 61, 50 hr | $13,498 | — |
| Air Fleet Pipistrel, Part 141, 35 hr floor | $11,076 | **−$2,422** |
| Air Fleet Pipistrel, Part 141, 45 hr | $13,604 | +$106 |
| Air Fleet Pipistrel, Part 141, 50 hr | $15,080 | +$1,582 |

**Break-even is about 45 hours.** Below that Air Fleet wins; above it Century Air does.

## 5. The downstream finding — this is larger than the private budget

**This is now an argument for Part 141 over Part 61, not for one school over the other** — both
offer Part 141. It remains the largest single number in the whole plan.

[Part 141 Appendix C](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/appendix-C_to_part_141), the
instrument rating course, has **no cross-country PIC hour requirement**. Its only enrollment
prerequisite is holding a private pilot certificate with the appropriate category and class
rating.

The [61.65(d)(1)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/61.65) requirement of **50 hours of
cross-country PIC** — identified earlier in this project as the real cost driver of the
instrument rating, worth roughly 24 extra hours and **$4,704** of pure rental — is a **Part 61
requirement only.** Flown as a Part 141 course end to end, that line disappears.

This is worth several times the private-certificate saving. Two conditions attach:

1. It must be a Part 141 instrument course **end to end**. Under
   [141.77](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/141.77) a Part 141 school may credit at most
   **50%** of a course from another Part 141 school, and **25%** from Part 61 training. Splitting
   the instrument rating between Air Fleet and USAA Denton runs straight into that cap.
2. It does not remove hours from the commercial certificate's 250-hour total (or 190 under
   Part 141 Appendix D) — it defers them to cheap solo PIC time instead of $280/hr dual.

## 6. What is NOT verified — ask before any of this is committed

### 6.1 Examining authority — their own sheet contradicts it

A Part 141 school with examining authority may recommend its graduates for a certificate
without an FAA knowledge test or practical test
([141.65](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/141.65)). It is granted **per course**, and
[141.63](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/141.63) sets a high bar: the school must have
trained at least 10 students in that course and had **at least 90% pass on the first attempt**.
Tests it gives must be at least equal in scope, depth and difficulty to the FAA's
([141.67](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/141.67)).

**But Air Fleet's own private pilot estimate budgets for both a $175 FAA written exam fee and a
"DPE Fee — Variable".** If they held and used examining authority on this course, neither line
would be there. Either they do not hold it for the private course, or they do not apply it by
default. Nothing on their public website mentions examining authority.

The value at stake is **$1,175** ($175 written + $1,000 DPE), less whatever internal end-of-course
test fee replaces it. Get it in writing, naming the course. Do not budget the saving until you do.

### 6.2 The Pipistrel is a light-sport aircraft

The [ALPHA Trainer POH](https://flypft.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Pipistrel-Alpha-Trainer-PIlot-Operating-Manual.pdf)
§2.15 approves VFR-day, VFR-night, and IFR in VMC only. Three consequences:

- **Night.** Appendix B requires 3 hours of night training including a >100 NM night cross-country
  and 10 takeoffs and landings. The POH's minimum equipment list (§2.15.1) lists navigation
  lights, anti-collision lights, landing light and instrument lights as *not required* even in
  the night column, deferring instead to "any additional equipment deemed necessary by local
  regulations". In the US that is [91.205(c)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/91.205).
  Whether night is legal in Air Fleet's actual airframe depends on that specific N-number's FAA
  operating limitations and its installed equipment. **Ask which tail number and ask to see the
  operating limitations.** If night has to be flown in a 172S, add roughly 3 hr × ($345 − $280) =
  **$195**, plus a separate checkout.
- **Instrument rating.** IFR in VMC only means the Alpha Trainer cannot be used for the instrument
  rating in actual conditions, and in practice not for the rating at all. The instrument phase
  returns to a 172.
- **Cabin load.** POH §3: max takeoff mass 550 kg / 1,212 lb, design empty weight 615 lb, design
  useful load 597 lb, usable fuel 48 L / 12.7 US gal ≈ 76 lb. That leaves roughly **520 lb for two
  occupants and baggage** with full fuel, against roughly 880 lb in a 172S. Endurance is 3 hours
  plus reserve. Confirm this works with your actual instructor's weight before committing — and
  note the POH figures are for the base Alpha Trainer, while the sheet lists the **Alpha Trainer
  PRO (G3X)**, which may differ.

### 6.4 The 30-hour ground school does not obviously meet Appendix B

Both schools told you 30 hours. [Appendix B §3](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/appendix-B_to_part_141)
sets the Part 141 floor at **35 hours** of ground training. Two legitimate explanations, and you
need to know which one applies:

1. **It is supplemented.** Air Fleet's price sheet shows a ~30-hour self-paced course plus
   **10 hours of live classroom instruction** — 40 hours total, comfortably over the floor. This
   is the most likely answer and it is what §3 of this document budgets.
2. **They hold an approved deviation.** [141.55(d)–(e)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/141.55)
   lets the Administrator approve a course that "does not meet the minimum ground and flight
   training time requirements", once the school has held its certificate 24 consecutive months
   and has trained at least 10 students in the course with **at least 80% passing on the first
   attempt**.

**Explanation 2 is worth chasing, because a deviation can cut flight hours too.** If either school
holds a §141.55(e) deviation, their approved private course may be **below 35 flight hours**, which
is cheaper than anything in §3's table. Ask directly: *"Is your private pilot course approved at
the Appendix B minimums, or do you hold a §141.55(e) deviation — and if so, what are the approved
ground and flight hours?"*

Note the thresholds differ: a deviation needs 80% first-time pass, examining authority
(§6.1) needs 90%. A school can hold one without the other.

### 6.3 Smaller items

- **Simulator fuel surcharge.** The surcharge reads "all aircraft except PIAT". A Redbird is not
  an aircraft and burns no fuel, but the sheet does not say so. Confirm the Redbird FMX is $90/hr
  flat, not $110.
- **Your existing Sporty's course.** Under Part 61,
  [61.105(a)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/61.105) accepts a home-study course. Under
  Part 141 the ground training must be part of the school's FAA-approved course outline, so your
  Sporty's course may not substitute for their $427.57 vendor course. Ask — it is only $427.57,
  but ask.
- **The $120/hr "own aircraft" instruction rate** is obscured by glare and appears in neither
  course total, so it could not be verified. It is not used in this budget.
- **Your FAA written.** It was booked for 9 September 2026 at Century Air. The knowledge test is
  an FAA test, portable between schools, valid 24 calendar months under
  [61.39(a)(1)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/61.39). Passing it does not remove the
  Part 141 ground training requirement.

## 7. Recommendation

**Enrol Part 141 on the Pipistrel, but budget 45–50 hours, not 35.**

The 35-hour column is the legal floor, not a plan. Air Fleet's own estimate of what the average
student actually takes is 65 hours, and the national average is higher still. A motivated,
self-studied student flying six booked days a week should plan around **45–50 hours** and treat
anything below 45 as upside. On that basis:

| | Air Fleet invoice | All-in |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Plan at 45 hr | $13,604.00 | **$15,206.57** |
| Plan at 50 hr | $15,080.50 | **$16,683.07** |
| Stretch, 35 hr floor | $11,076.00 | $12,678.57 |
| If examining authority is real, deduct | — | −$1,175.00 |

### Why Air Fleet, now that Part 141 is available at both

The original version of this document recommended Air Fleet because of the Part 141 instrument
advantage. That reasoning no longer separates the two schools — Century Air offers Part 141 too,
so §5 is worth roughly $4,700 either way. What is left is a straight trade:

| | Century Air 172P | Air Fleet Pipistrel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cost at matched hours | **$1,000–$1,160 cheaper** | — |
| Part 141 available | Yes | Yes |
| Instrument rating without 50 hr XC PIC | Yes, under 141 | Yes, under 141 |
| Cheapest time-builder | 172P solo $196/hr | **Pipistrel $195/hr wet** |
| Cadence — days per week, doubles | Unproven; a standing six-day pattern was still an open ask | **Confirmed flexible in person: more days, two flights a day** |
| Your own read of the place | Did not like it | Preferred it |

**Choose Air Fleet.** The price gap is real but small — about four hours of dual across the whole
course. Cadence is the thing that decides whether this is a 45-hour course or a 65-hour one, and
that is worth several times $1,160. Buying the school that will actually fly you six days a week
with doubles, rather than the one that is $1,158 cheaper on paper, is the correct trade at these
numbers.

The two soft factors point the same way, which matters: a school you do not enjoy is a school you
cancel on, and cancelling is exactly what the cadence model cannot absorb.

**Three things still to confirm in writing before money moves:** the examining-authority claim
(§6.1, worth $1,175), night legality in the actual Pipistrel airframe (§6.2), and whether either
school holds a §141.55(e) deviation that would put the approved course below 35 flight hours
(§6.4, potentially worth more than the school choice itself).
